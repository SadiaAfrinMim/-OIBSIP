The Woman Who Unlocked the Atom

Marie Skłodowska Curie was born in Warsaw, Poland, in 1867, at a time when higher education was largely denied to women. Undeterred, she moved to Paris and enrolled at the Sorbonne, where she earned degrees in physics and mathematics. It was there she met Pierre Curie, a fellow scientist who would become both her husband and her closest collaborator. Together, they embarked on a journey of scientific inquiry that would forever change humanity's understanding of matter and energy.

In 1898, the Curies announced the discovery of two new elements: polonium, named after Marie's homeland, and radium, named for its intense radioactivity. Their work challenged prevailing ideas about the atom and opened entirely new fields of physics and chemistry. Marie Curie coined the term "radioactivity" to describe the phenomenon, laying linguistic and conceptual foundations that scientists still rely on today.

Beyond the laboratory, Marie Curie broke barriers in academia, becoming the first woman to win a Nobel Prize and remains the only person ever to win Nobel Prizes in two different scientific fields — Physics (1903) and Chemistry (1911). During World War I, she developed mobile radiography units, known as "Petites Curies," which brought X-ray technology to battlefield hospitals and saved countless lives.

Her legacy endures not only in the scientific advancements she inspired, but also in the generations of women she empowered to pursue careers in science. Marie Curie died in 1934 from aplastic anemia, likely caused by years of radiation exposure, but her notebooks remain radioactive to this day, sealed in lead-lined boxes — a testament to the monumental impact of her work.

Two Nobels, One Legend

The only person to win Nobel Prizes in two different sciences

1903 Physics

Nobel Prize in Physics

Awarded jointly with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel "in recognition of the extraordinary services they have rendered by their joint researches on the radiation phenomena discovered by Professor Henri Becquerel."

1911 Chemistry

Nobel Prize in Chemistry

Awarded "in recognition of her services to the advancement of chemistry by the discovery of the elements radium and polonium, by the isolation of radium and the study of the nature and compounds of this remarkable element."

Key Achievements

  1. 1867 Born in Warsaw, Poland, as Maria Skłodowska.
  2. 1891 Moved to Paris and enrolled at the Sorbonne (University of Paris).
  3. 1895 Married Pierre Curie; began collaborative research on radioactivity.
  4. 1898 Discovered the elements polonium and radium with Pierre Curie.
  5. 1903 Awarded the Nobel Prize in Physics (shared with Pierre Curie and Henri Becquerel).
  6. 1906 Appointed Professor of Physics at the Sorbonne — the first woman to hold the position.
  7. 1911 Awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for the discovery of radium and polonium.
  8. 1914–1918 Developed mobile X-ray units ("Petites Curies") during World War I.
  9. 1934 Died in Passy, France; her legacy continues to shape modern science.
